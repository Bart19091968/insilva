What SIAM is
SIAM is a management methodology for creating value in an ecosystem where multiple service providers — internal, external, or both — work together to deliver services to a single customer organization.
service integration + management = SIAM

A framework, not a prescription

SIAM is not
one-size-fits-all.

It is a comprehensive framework of guidelines, best practices, concepts and models — designed to be applied flexibly to suit the needs of your specific multi-sourced ecosystem.

What SIAM gives you is a shared vocabulary and a set of well-understood building blocks. What you build with them depends on your organization, your providers, and the outcomes you need.

Same blocks. Different buildings.

Layer · 02 · Key concept

Service
integrator.

02SERVICE
INTEGRATOR

A logical entity — one team or several, internal or external — held accountable for the end-to-end delivery of services across the ecosystem. The conductor of the orchestra.

Five-fold accountability

  • Governance
  • Management
  • Integration
  • Assurance
  • Coordination

Sits between

  • The customer organization (Layer 01)
  • The service providers (Layer 03)
  • One logical entity — possibly many teams
  • Independent from any retained capability

Sourcing the integrator
15 / 32
Structure · 01

Internally sourced
service integrator.

01INTERNALLY
SOURCED

The customer organization plays the integrator role itself. Maximum control and ownership — at the cost of having to build and sustain the capability in-house.

Advantages

  • Direct control and accountability
  • No third party in the middle
  • Independence from any provider's commercial interests

Disadvantages

  • Must build and maintain integration capability
  • Internal providers may resist internal authority
  • Slower to stand up than buying-in expertise
In Silva · SIAM Foundation
Best when capability already exists in-house
Sourcing the integrator
16 / 32
Structure · 02

Externally sourced
service integrator.

02EXTERNALLY
SOURCED

An external organization is appointed to be the integrator. Mature expertise, fast. But authority is only as strong as the customer's willingness to back it.

Advantages

  • Buys in proven integration capability
  • Faster to stand up than building internally
  • Independent from service providers

Disadvantages

  • No authority over providers without customer backing
  • Additional cost and contract to manage
  • Customer still needs strong retained capabilities
In Silva · SIAM Foundation
Empower it, or it becomes a paper tiger
Sourcing the integrator
17 / 32
Structure · 03

Lead supplier as
service integrator.

03LEAD
SUPPLIER

One service provider — usually the largest — also plays integrator. Simpler contracting, but watch for the conflict of interest baked into the model.

Advantages

  • One prime contract, fewer interfaces
  • Existing relationship and domain knowledge
  • Useful as a transitional structure

Disadvantages

  • Inherent conflict: assuring work you also deliver
  • Bias toward own services, away from competitors
  • Lock-in risk — switching costs grow over time
In Silva · SIAM Foundation
Manage the bias, or it manages you
Sourcing the integrator
18 / 32
Structure · 04

Hybrid service
integrator.

04HYBRID

The customer collaborates with one or more external organizations to share the integrator role. Powerful and flexible — and the most demanding to design well.

Advantages

  • Combines internal context with external expertise
  • No single party needs every capability
  • Customer stays close to the work

Disadvantages

  • Hardest structure to design and govern
  • Requires clear boundaries and escalation paths
  • Accountability can blur if not actively maintained

Working groups.

Time-boxed teams stood up to solve a specific problem — then disbanded.

Four practice areas

People, process, measurement, technology.

Practice · 01

People.

Managing cross-functional teams and fostering collaboration across organizational boundaries.

Practice · 02

Process.

Integrating processes end-to-end — so a change or an incident moves cleanly across every provider it touches.

Practice · 03

Measurement.

Reporting on end-to-end service experience, not per-provider SLAs — fixing the "watermelon" effect.

Practice · 04

Technology.

A tooling strategy that lets data and information flow across the ecosystem — one toolset, integrated, or aggregated.

Challenges & risks

SIAM is hard. Do it deliberately.

Challenge · 01

Building the business case.

Quantifying value across a multi-provider ecosystem with intangible benefits.

Challenge · 02

Cultural change.

Shifting from command-and-control to collaboration. Behavior change at scale.

Challenge · 03

Commercial alignment.

Contracts, incentives and pricing models that actually reward integrated outcomes.

Challenge · 04

Measuring success.

Building end-to-end performance reporting that spans multiple providers.

Challenge · 05

Tooling & data.

Integrating disparate toolsets so information flows cleanly across the ecosystem.

Challenge · 06

Governance discipline.

Keeping boards, forums and working groups productive — not just calendar entries.
