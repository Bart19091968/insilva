What ITSM means
ITSM is the set of processes an organization follows to design, plan, deliver, operate and control the IT services it offers to its end‑users.
design → plan → deliver → operate → control
ITSM = IT Service Management

Examples in everyday work

You've probably already used it.

EX. 01
"I need a new laptop for the new hire starting Monday."
That's a service request. Something a user needs — hardware, software, access — fulfilled through a defined process.
EX. 02
"The Wi‑Fi is down on the third floor."
That's an incident. A service that should be working isn't, and someone needs to get it back up — fast.

A framework for best practices

ITIL.

I
Information
T
Technology
I
Infrastructure

ITIL is a set of best practices for ITSM, focused on aligning IT services with business needs. It offers guidance on planning, delivering and managing services so they're efficient, effective and meet what the organization actually needs.
If ITSM is the practice, ITIL is the playbook.

BENEFITS
11 / 13
Six benefits of ITSM

Each one reinforces the next.

BENEFIT 01

Consistent, structured processes.

Defined workflows reduce errors, cut downtime, and lift overall efficiency — from incident resolution to change rollout.

BENEFIT 02

Higher service quality.

Services are delivered reliably and consistently. SLAs are met, issues caught proactively, incidents resolved faster.

BENEFIT 03

Cost efficiency.

Standardized procedures cut the need for ad-hoc fixes and repeated effort — less waste, less unnecessary spend.

BENEFIT 04

Customer satisfaction.

Fewer disruptions, better service, more trust in the IT department. A positive experience across the organization.

BENEFIT 05

Continuous improvement.

Frameworks like ITIL bake in regular review and refinement — the practice keeps getting sharper over time.

BENEFIT 06

Aligned with business goals.

IT services support what the business is actually trying to do — and the highest-value initiatives get prioritized.

Compounding effects
